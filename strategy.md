# The Whiskey Student: content strategy

The plan for the channel: positioning, pillars, series, production, distillery visits and learning resources. Edit it any time.

## 1. Positioning

The angle is simple and hard to copy: a working bartender and professional photographer documenting the real path from curious to expert. Established whiskey channels sell authority. You sell the climb. Every piece of content should either teach the audience something you just learned, or show the moment you learned it.

### Brand promises

- **Honest over polished.** Unscripted first reactions stay in. Mistakes become episodes.

- **Learn with me.** The audience is a classmate, not a customer. Speak to “us,” not “you.”

- **Behind the bar credibility.** You can tell people how a whiskey works in a cocktail and what guests actually order. Use that in every category.

- **Shot like a pro.** Your photography is the visual signature: warm amber light, macro pours, clean bar top. Keep one consistent look.

## 4. Production Schedule

Batch everything. Shoot 2–3 weeks ahead of posting so a busy bar week never breaks the schedule. Plan production around your bar shifts; adjust days to match your rotation.

### Weekly rhythm

| **Day**       | **Block**          | **Time**   | **What happens**                                                                                                                         |
|---------------|--------------------|------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Sunday**    | Batch Shoot        | 3–4 hrs    | Film 4–5 pieces in one lighting setup: 2 tastings, 1 Blind Friday, 1 Whiskey in 60, stills for a carousel. Change shirts between pieces. |
| **Monday**    | Edit Reels         | 2 hrs      | Cut the week’s Reels. Captions burned in. Export clean (no app watermarks).                                                              |
| **Tuesday**   | Study + POV        | 1–2 hrs    | WSET reading. Film Study Hall on your phone while you study. Log notes in journal.                                                       |
| **Wednesday** | Edit Long-form     | 2–3 hrs    | YouTube cut, thumbnail shoot (2 options), title + description with keywords.                                                             |
| **Thursday**  | Package & Schedule | 1 hr       | Write captions, schedule in Meta Business Suite and YouTube Studio, plan Stories.                                                        |
| **Fri – Sat** | Bar shifts         | On the job | Capture 1–2 Behind the Bar clips only when it doesn’t interfere with service.                                                            |
| **Daily**     | Community          | 15 min     | Reply to comments and DMs in the first hour after each post.                                                                             |

### Monthly rhythm

| **Week**   | **Focus**                 | **Output**                                                                 |
|------------|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Week 1** | Plan + big batch shoot    | Lock the month’s calendar, buy bottles, shoot 2 weeks of tastings.         |
| **Week 2** | Distillery visit          | 1 trip = 1 YT vlog, 5–7 Reels, 1 carousel, 15+ Stories, 40+ stills.        |
| **Week 3** | Education + history batch | Shoot Whiskey in 60 and Lost Bottles episodes; record voiceovers.          |
| **Week 4** | Review + prep             | Check analytics, re-taste a past bottle for Then vs. Now, plan next month. |

### Kit checklist

- Main camera + 50mm/macro lens for pours; phone on gimbal for POV and distillery walk-throughs.

- Wireless lav mic (needed for distillery interviews and loud tasting rooms).

- One key light with softbox + amber practical light for the signature look.

- Glencairn glasses (set of 4), label covers or numbered sleeves for blind tastings, dump bucket, water dropper.

- Tasting journal (physical, photographed for carousels) plus a spreadsheet log for your Whiskey Counter.

### Reel recipe

- **0–3 sec:** Hook line on screen and spoken. Show the glass or bottle immediately.

- **3–45 sec:** One idea only. Close-ups every 2–3 seconds. Burned-in captions.

- **End:** Verdict or question that invites a comment (“Which would you pick?”). Design the last frame to loop into the first.

- **Length:** Most Reels 30–60 seconds; history stories up to 90. Stay under 3 minutes so non-followers can be served it.

- **Caption:** First line = searchable keywords (“Maryland rye whiskey review”). Then 2–3 sentences of story. 3–5 specific hashtags max.

### YouTube recipe

- **Titles:** Search phrase first, personality second. “Best Bourbon Under $40? A Bartender Tries 5 Blind.”

- **Thumbnails:** Your face + glass + 3 words max. Shoot them on purpose; you’re a photographer, this is your edge.

- **Structure:** Payoff promised in first 20 seconds, chapters in description, end screen to the next video in the series.

- **Playlists:** One per series and one per category (Bourbon, Scotch, Irish, Japanese, ASM, Maryland Rye).

## 7. Distillery Visit Playbook

### Before the visit

- Email the distillery 2–3 weeks ahead: introduce the channel, ask permission to film, request 10 minutes with a distiller or guide.

- Ask what areas are off-limits for cameras and whether barrel sampling is allowed.

- Prepare one interview question and a shot list. Bring signed location/talent release forms.

- Book a designated driver or rideshare. Never film or post yourself driving after tasting.

### Shot list (per visit)

| **Shot**                                                 | **Use**                                         |
|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| **Arrival exterior, sign, wide establishing**            | Opening of vlog, carousel cover                 |
| **Grain, mill, mash tun, fermenters**                    | Grain to Glass in 60; education B-roll          |
| **Still (wide + copper macro)**                          | Pot vs. Column episode; thumbnails              |
| **Barrel warehouse, 60s ambient audio**                  | Rickhouse Sounds ASMR Reel                      |
| **Distiller interview (lav mic, vertical + horizontal)** | One Question Reel + YT segment                  |
| **Tasting flight, overhead + 45° pours**                 | Reels, carousel, thumbnail                      |
| **Gift shop exclusive bottle**                           | Second review video at home                     |
| **Your reactions (phone POV)**                           | Stories and the honest “Worth the Ticket?” Reel |

### Content yield per trip

1 YouTube vlog (12–20 min) · 5–7 Reels · 1–2 Shorts · 1 photo carousel · 1 “Plan Your Visit” guide carousel · 15+ Stories · 40+ edited stills (also usable as portfolio for your media company).

## 8. Learning Resources

### Certifications (the credibility arc)

| **Program**                                           | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|-------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **WSET Level 2 Award in Spirits**                     | Your central milestone. Covers production and all major spirit categories, tasting with the WSET Systematic Approach, and cocktails. 50-question multiple-choice exam. Online options include Commonwealth Wine School (an Oct–Dec 2026 cohort with an online exam on Dec 6) and Napa Valley Wine Academy’s on-demand course (online exam date listed for Dec 9, 2026). Check current booking deadlines. |
| **WSET Level 3 Award in Spirits**                     | Next step in 2027. Adds a blind tasting exam, which makes great content.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Stave & Thief Society — Certified Bourbon Steward** | Bourbon-focused certification, popular with hospitality pros. A good complement for American whiskey.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Moonshine University (Louisville)**                 | Hands-on distilling and whiskey courses; pairs well with a Kentucky trip.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Edinburgh Whisky Academy**                          | Scotch-focused diplomas, available online. Strong for Scotch month.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

### Books

- **The World Atlas of Whisky — Dave Broom.** The global reference; read country by country as your roadmap moves.

- **Whiskey Distilled — Heather Greene.** Approachable tasting-first guide; great for the beginner arc.

- **Bourbon Curious — Fred Minnick.** Bourbon myths, production, and history.

- **American Whiskey, Bourbon & Rye — Clay Risen.** Bottle-by-bottle field guide for reviews.

- **The Bourbon Empire — Reid Mitenbuler.** History and business of American whiskey; mine it for Lost Bottles episodes.

- **Japanese Whisky — Brian Ashcraft.** History and producers for Japanese month.

### Podcasts & channels

- Whiskey Lore (Drew Hannush) — history-heavy; also has a profile page for Maryland distilleries.

- Bourbon Pursuit — industry interviews and news.

- WhiskyCast (Mark Gillespie) — long-running global whisky news.

- Whisky Advocate (magazine + site) — reviews, ratings, travel guides.

### Primary sources (the rules)

- U.S. TTB Standards of Identity for whisky (bourbon, rye, Tennessee, bottled-in-bond, American Single Malt).

- The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 and the Scotch Whisky Association site.

- Irish Whiskey Technical File (Irish Whiskey Association).

- Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association labeling standards for “Japanese Whisky.”

- American Single Malt Whiskey Commission (category advocacy and member list).

### Training tools

- Whisky aroma kit for blind nose training (monthly Aroma Kit Blind episode).

- Flavor wheel printed and pinned behind your tasting setup.

- Tasting log spreadsheet: date, bottle, category, proof, price, notes, Buy/Bar Pour/Pass.

| **Follower growth from content**   | IG + YT     | Which pillar actually converts viewers.   |

Each month: double down on the top 2 formats by sends and watch time, cut the weakest one, and test one new idea.

### Responsible-content rules

- Set your Instagram account’s audience age restriction to 21+ for alcohol content (check the setting under professional/branded content controls).

- Taste, don’t drink: small pours, dump bucket visible sometimes, water on the table. Never glamorize heavy drinking or drinking and driving.

- Disclose every free bottle, sample, comped tour, or paid deal (#ad, “gifted,” or the platform’s paid partnership label).

- Get written permission to film inside distilleries and signed releases for anyone featured, including bar guests.

- Keep content clearly educational and honest. That protects the brand and your future work with spirits clients.
